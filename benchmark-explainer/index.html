This is division, not a scaling forecast. Network, storage, replication, side effects, and tail latency must be load-tested in your deployment.

WHY IT MATTERS

It budgets orchestration overhead

An end-to-end workflow rate is useful when estimating how much durable coordination can fit before business logic and external APIs become the bottleneck.

WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE

Your workflow will reach 54K/s

The measured handlers and environment differ from your payloads, side effects, indexes, network, storage, and reliability topology. No latency percentile is implied.

CORRECT COMPARISON

Workflow completion vs. workflow completion

Do not compare one completed workflow—which can contain several durable operations—to one Stream entry, SQL write, or HTTP request.

WHY IT MATTERS

It exposes hot-shard limits

The topology changes how much durable work can proceed in parallel. The best result came from sufficiently large batches distributed across 12 partition Streams.

WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE

More partitions always win

Interleaving or forcing many topics onto one shard produced much lower rates. On one host, extra groups can add durability overhead instead of capacity.

CORRECT COMPARISON

Match the acknowledgement boundary

This path includes a one-node Raft command, deterministic projection, and local fsynced WAL. Compare only with systems configured for an equivalent success boundary.

The logical delivery count stays the same. The optimization changes batching and encoding, not the meaning or order of the messages.

WHY IT MATTERS

Fanout multiplies outbound work

A publish-only number hides subscriber delivery cost. Capacity planning needs both the input rate and total delivered messages across subscribers.

WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE

WAN or subscriber processing speed

The run used loopback TCP and validated delivery counts. It does not measure remote networks, application callbacks, or durable replay—Pub/Sub is live fanout.

CORRECT COMPARISON

Keep fanout and event format fixed

Comparing fanout 1 with fanout 8, or individual frames with negotiated batches, changes the work. Report both publishes/s and deliveries/s.

BEFORE TRUSTING A CHART

Ask four questions

01

What completed?

A workflow, batch request, Stream entry, publish, and subscriber delivery are different units.

02

What was durable?

Match replication, WAL/fsync, acknowledgement, and ordering semantics before comparing systems.

03

What shaped the load?

Keep payload, concurrency, batching, shards, fanout, retention, and resource guards visible.

04

Where is the tail?

Peak throughput without p95/p99 latency can hide storage stalls and queueing under saturation.
